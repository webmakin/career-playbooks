# VP of Engineering Playbook

## Preface: How to Read This Book

> **The VPE role is a category change, not a title change.** This book is for the Director or senior IC who is making the shift to VPE — and the sitting VPE who wants to design the systems they own.

### Who this book is for

**Primary reader:** A Director, senior EM, or staff IC who is targeting the VPE role in the next 12-24 months. You're either interviewing, in the offer stage, or 0-6 months into the VPE seat.

**Secondary readers:**
- Sits VPEs who want to design the systems they own (hiring, perf, risk, audit, etc.).
- CTOs who want to understand the VPE-side playbook.
- Founders/CEOs hiring their first VPE.

### What this book is not

- **Not a CTO playbook.** The CTO owns tech strategy. The VPE owns engineering execution. Different roles. (See cross-references to the AI Eng Director playbook for the AI-flavored VPE track.)
- **Not a Director playbook.** The Director owns a domain. The VPE owns a portfolio. Different scope.
- **Not a manager-EPM playbook.** The VPE is the last technical person in the room. The VPE does not write code daily, but the VPE must understand the systems.

### How to read this book

**Reading path A (aspirant, 12-24 months from the role):**
- Read Part I (Foundations, Ch 1-4) first.
- Read Part V (Leadership at scale, Ch 18-21) to understand the VPE's most leveraged systems.
- Read Part VII (The VPE's Portfolio, Ch 26-28) to build your portfolio for the next interview.
- Skim the other parts for context.

**Reading path B (in-seat, 0-6 months into the role):**
- Read Part VII first (Ch 26 30/60/90 is your day-1 plan).
- Read Part V (Leadership, Ch 18-21) to design your org, hiring, perf, and C-suite relationships.
- Read Part VI (Governance & Risk, Ch 22-25) to design your risk register, regulatory posture, crisis response, and auditability.
- Read Part IV (Product & Strategy, Ch 14-17) for the VPE-CPO partnership, strategy, and build-vs-buy.

**Reading path C (Director-level EM, 6-12 months from the VPE seat):**
- Read Part II (Engineering Spine, Ch 5-9) for the technical governance frameworks.
- Read Part III (Platform & Production, Ch 10-13) for the platform engineering, reliability, and security models.
- Read Part V (Leadership, Ch 18-21) to design the org and perf systems.

### How to use the drills

Each chapter has a 90-minute drill (`exercises/chapterNN/drill.md`). The drill is a fictional scenario at acme-corp (a 1,200-person B2B SaaS company). The drill produces a 1-page artifact (`portfolio/chapter-NN-{slug}.md`) using 3 frameworks from the chapter.

**Grading:** Each chapter's Self-Assessment Rubric (5 dimensions, 25 points, pass at 18+) tells you how to score your own artifact. If you score <18/25, re-draft using the chapter's mental models and frameworks.

**Portfolio:** Save the 28 artifacts as `portfolio/chapter-NN-{slug}.md`. The portfolio is your interview evidence (Ch 27) and your first-90-days plan (Ch 26).

### Voice and tone

This book is opinionated, direct, and first-person. The author has seen VPEs succeed and fail. The book tells you what works and what doesn't. The book is not a survey of best practices — it is a set of bets on what the VPE role actually requires at company scale.

### Conventions

- **acme-corp** is the fictional company in every drill.
- **The VPE** is "you" in every drill.
- **Directors** are senior leaders reporting to the VPE.
- **Framework X, Y, Z** in each chapter are the 3 reusable templates.
